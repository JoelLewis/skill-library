# Sharpened Quarterly Strategy Update

Your draft has three problems: vague qualifiers ("good," "well," "mixed"), no numbers, and no clear ask. Leadership updates should quantify results, name decisions, and state what you need from the room. Here is a rewrite.

---

**Q1 Results**

- **Dashboard launch:** Shipped Feb 3. 1,200 active users in first 6 weeks, [X]% of target. [Insert key usage metric — e.g., daily sessions, feature adoption rate.]
- **API redesign:** On track for [milestone] by [date]. [X] of [Y] endpoints migrated. No blockers.
- **Pricing changes (Jan):** NPS dropped [X] points among mid-market accounts. [X]% of support tickets in Q1 referenced pricing. Churn in this segment up [X]% QoQ.

**Q2 Priorities**

1. **Retention:** Reduce monthly churn from [X]% to [X]% by [specific action — e.g., launching a win-back campaign, revising the mid-market tier, fixing the three most-cited pain points].
2. **Vertical expansion:** Enter [name the vertical]. TAM is $[X]M. First pilot with [customer or segment] by [date].
3. **Competitive response:** [Name the competitor or shift]. Our exposure is [specific gap]. Mitigation: [specific action and owner].

**What I need from this group**

- Decision on whether to revise mid-market pricing or hold through Q2.
- Headcount approval for [X] roles to support vertical expansion.
- Alignment on competitive positioning — proposal circulated by [date].

---

## What changed and why

**Replace vague qualifiers with numbers.** "Good adoption" means nothing to a leadership audience. Even placeholder brackets ([X]%) force you to find the actual figure before presenting, which is the point. A number you are embarrassed by is more useful than an adjective that sounds fine.

**Name the decisions.** "Focus on retention" is a wish, not a strategy. Saying *how* you will improve retention (which lever, which segment, which timeline) turns it into something the room can approve, challenge, or resource.

**Add an explicit ask.** Every leadership update should end with what you need from the people in the room. If you walk out without a decision or commitment, the update was informational, not strategic.

**Cut the throat-clearing.** "Over the past quarter, the team has been working on several initiatives" tells the reader nothing. Start with results.

**Be specific about competition.** "The competitive landscape is shifting and we need to stay ahead" applies to every company in every quarter. Name the threat, name the gap, name the response.
