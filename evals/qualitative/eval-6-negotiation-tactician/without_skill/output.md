# Preparing for Your AWS Meeting Tomorrow

## Understand Your Position First

You are in a stronger position than you might think. AWS is approaching you -- they want your business. That $100k credit offer is a customer acquisition cost for them, and the account exec has a quota to hit. Keep that framing in mind throughout the meeting.

## Key Numbers to Have Ready

- **Your current spend**: $8k/month ($96k/year) on a competitor's cloud
- **AWS's ask**: $15k/month ($180k/year) after credits expire -- nearly double your current spend
- **Credit runway**: $100k in credits at $15k/month equivalent usage lasts roughly 6-7 months, not even a full year
- **Total 3-year cost after credits**: Approximately $540k (36 months x $15k), minus the $100k credit, so $440k net
- **3-year cost staying put**: Approximately $288k (36 months x $8k), assuming no growth

Write these numbers down before the meeting. The AWS offer sounds generous because $100k is a big number, but the total commitment over 3 years is significantly more expensive than your current setup.

## Preparation Steps

### 1. Clarify Your Actual Needs

Before the meeting, answer these questions for yourself:

- Why are you considering AWS at all? Is there a specific service (ML, managed databases, global CDN) your current provider lacks?
- What is your projected growth in cloud spend over the next 12-24 months?
- Are there migration costs (engineering time, downtime risk) to factor in?

If you don't have a genuine technical reason to move, the credits alone aren't a reason to switch. Migration has real costs that eat into that $100k quickly.

### 2. Research Competing Offers

Contact Google Cloud and Azure before or shortly after the meeting. Both have startup credit programs:

- **Google Cloud for Startups**: Up to $200k in credits (through accelerator partnerships)
- **Microsoft for Startups (Founders Hub)**: Up to $150k in Azure credits

Even if you don't plan to use them, having competing offers gives you leverage. You can mention you're evaluating multiple providers without bluffing.

Also check if your current provider has a retention or growth program. Tell them AWS is courting you -- they may offer credits or discounts to keep you.

### 3. Know What to Push Back On

The $15k/month commitment is the main issue. Your pushback points:

- **Your current spend is $8k/month.** Committing to nearly double that assumes growth you may not hit. Ask: "Can we tie the commitment to actual usage rather than a fixed floor?"
- **3 years is a long lock-in for a startup.** Startups pivot. Ask for a 1-year commitment with renewal options, or a ramp-up schedule (e.g., $8k/month year 1, $12k/month year 2, $15k/month year 3).
- **The credits should offset commitment, not precede it.** Propose that the $100k in credits apply against the first months of your enterprise agreement, not as a separate "free trial" that hooks you into an expensive contract.

### 4. Tactics for the Meeting Itself

**Don't decide in the room.** Your single most important rule. No matter how good the offer sounds, say: "This is helpful. I need to discuss with my co-founder/board/advisor before committing." This gives you time to evaluate without pressure.

**Ask questions, don't negotiate yet.** Treat this meeting as information gathering:

- "What does the enterprise agreement actually lock us into? Is it a spend commitment or a contract term?"
- "What happens if our usage drops below $15k/month -- do we still pay the difference?"
- "Are credits applied before or after the commitment calculation?"
- "What flexibility exists on the term length?"
- "Can we start with a shorter pilot before signing a 3-year deal?"

**Let them sell.** The more they talk, the more you learn about their flexibility. Account execs often reveal what's negotiable when they're in pitch mode.

**Name the gap directly.** At some point, say something like: "We're spending $8k/month today and it's working. For us to move, the economics need to make sense beyond the initial credits. A $15k/month floor is hard to justify to our board when our current run rate is half that."

### 5. Your Best Possible Outcome

A realistic good deal might look like:

- $100k in credits with a 12-month or 24-month expiry (not 6 months)
- A 1-year enterprise agreement at $8-10k/month commitment, with option to renew
- Or a 3-year agreement with a ramp: $8k year 1, $10k year 2, $12-15k year 3
- Commitment based on actual usage, not a fixed minimum

## What to Watch Out For

- **Urgency pressure**: "This offer expires Friday" or "I can only hold this pricing through end of quarter." These are standard tactics. The offer will still be there next week.
- **Bundling complexity**: Adding services you don't need to justify the higher spend. Stick to what you actually use today plus what you concretely plan to use in the next 6 months.
- **Comparing apples to oranges**: AWS pricing structures differ from other clouds. Make sure any comparison is on equivalent services and configurations.
- **Credit expiration terms**: Read the fine print on when credits expire and what services they apply to. Some credits exclude certain high-cost services.

## Bottom Line

Go in curious, not committed. You have leverage because you're a growing startup with a working infrastructure elsewhere, and AWS wants you on their platform before you scale further on a competitor. The $100k is their investment in acquiring you as a long-term customer -- make sure the long-term terms actually work for your business before accepting.
