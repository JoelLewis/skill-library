# Structural Diagnosis: Climate Scientist Novel

## The Core Problem

Your first act has an 80-page runway with no value turns before the discovery. In McKee's terms, those pages contain "exposition as scene" — activity without events. Information is being delivered, but nothing is changing. The reader has no reason to lean forward because no value has been placed at risk. (Source: McKee, *Story*, Ch. 14)

## Diagnosis Using the Skill Frameworks

### 1. Missing Inciting Incident (McKee / Storr)

The discovery of manipulation on page 80 is functioning as your Inciting Incident — the moment of "Unexpected Change" that throws the protagonist's life out of balance and opens a Curiosity Gap. But page 80 is far too late. In a standard novel, the Inciting Incident should land within the first 10-15% of the manuscript. For a 300-page novel, that means by page 30-45 at the latest.

The real question: what is your protagonist's life *before* the discovery, and what event disrupts it? The manipulation discovery should not be the Inciting Incident. It should be the **Act I Climax** — the point of no return.

### 2. The Sacred Flaw Is Not Under Pressure (Storr)

Your climate scientist has a "theory of control" — a flawed belief about how the world works. Based on your premise, her Sacred Flaw is likely something like: *"Institutional science is trustworthy, and doing good work within the system will produce honest results."* Or perhaps: *"If I follow the methodology, the truth will protect itself."*

The problem with your current structure is that nothing challenges this belief until page 80. The plot needs to begin pressuring her flaw from the opening chapters — not by revealing the manipulation, but by creating small cracks in her certainty that something feels wrong.

### 3. No ABT Causal Chain (Industrial Scripts)

Your first act reads as pure "And" — *she does research, AND she goes to the lab, AND she talks to colleagues, AND she reviews data.* There is no "But" creating friction before page 80. The ABT framework demands conflict much earlier:

**Current structure:**
- She is a climate scientist AND she does her work AND she discovers manipulation on page 80.

**What it needs to be:**
- She is a climate scientist doing important work (AND), BUT small anomalies in the data don't add up (BUT), THEREFORE she begins quietly investigating (THEREFORE) — which leads to the full discovery at the Act I Climax.

### 4. Value Audit Failure (McKee)

Running McKee's value audit on your first act:

| Scene/Sequence | Value at Stake | Does It Turn? |
|---|---|---|
| Pages 1-20 (setup) | ? | Likely no turn — pure exposition |
| Pages 20-50 (research life) | ? | Likely no turn — routine depicted |
| Pages 50-80 (more setup) | ? | Likely no turn — building to discovery |
| Page 80 (discovery) | Trust/Betrayal | Yes — first real turn |

You have one turn in 80 pages. You need turns in every sequence. The core value at stake in this novel is **Trust vs. Betrayal** (or **Truth vs. Deception**). That value needs to oscillate from the first chapter.

## The Fix: Restructured Act I

### Move 1: New Inciting Incident (by page 10-15)

Open with an event of unexpected change that is *not* the manipulation reveal but that disturbs her equilibrium. Options:

- **A data anomaly she can't explain.** One of her own published results doesn't replicate. This is personally threatening (her reputation) and professionally strange. It opens a Curiosity Gap: why doesn't this replicate?
- **A colleague's quiet departure.** Someone on her team leaves abruptly — no explanation, no farewell. This is social disruption that creates unease without revealing the conspiracy.
- **An outside journalist contacts her.** Someone asks pointed questions about her university's funding sources. She dismisses it, but the reader now has a question the protagonist doesn't.

Each of these creates the "But" in the ABT chain and puts her Sacred Flaw under immediate pressure. She believes the institution is trustworthy — and now something doesn't fit.

### Move 2: Escalating Road of Trials (pages 15-70)

Using Campbell's framework, the middle of Act I becomes a series of small threshold crossings, each testing her flaw:

1. **She investigates the anomaly casually** — tells herself it's a simple error. (Sacred Flaw intact.)
2. **The anomaly deepens.** She finds a second irregularity. A colleague deflects her questions. (First crack in the flaw.)
3. **She crosses a threshold.** She accesses records she wouldn't normally look at — a minor act of transgression that signals she's leaving her "Known World" of comfortable institutional trust. (Campbell: Crossing the First Threshold.)
4. **She encounters resistance.** Her department chair redirects her, praises her work, offers a new grant — all of which feel slightly wrong. (Antagonistic forces escalate per McKee's Principle of Antagonism.)
5. **She confides in someone and is warned off.** The stakes become personal — someone she trusts tells her to stop looking. (Value turn: Safety to Danger.)

Each of these sequences turns a value. Trust erodes incrementally. The reader watches the protagonist's certainty crack before the full revelation.

### Move 3: Act I Climax — The Discovery (pages 70-80)

Now the manipulation discovery on page 80 functions as the **Act I Climax** — McKee's "point of no return." It works because:

- The reader has been anticipating it (Sanderson's Promise has been set and Progress shown).
- It represents a major value reversal: from "something feels off" to "my entire body of work may be fraudulent."
- It forces the protagonist out of her old world permanently (Campbell: she cannot un-know this).
- It demands a response — launching Act II with a clear Object of Desire (expose the truth, or protect herself, or both).

### Move 4: Promise/Progress/Payoff Alignment (Sanderson)

Your opening pages make a **Promise** to the reader about what kind of story this is. If you open with 80 pages of quiet academic life, you're promising a literary character study. If the manipulation reveal then pivots into a thriller structure, you've broken the promise.

Fix the promise by seeding tension from the start:

- **Promise (pages 1-15):** Something is wrong in this institution. This is a story about a person who will have to choose between comfort and truth.
- **Progress (pages 15-75):** She moves closer to the truth through escalating discovery, each step costing her something — a friendship, a sense of safety, her confidence in her own work.
- **Payoff (page 80, Act I Climax):** The full scope of the manipulation revealed. The promise is fulfilled; a larger promise is made for Acts II and III.

## Diagnostic Checklist Applied

- [x] **Does the story begin with an event of unexpected change?** It will, once you add the Inciting Incident by page 10-15.
- [x] **Is there a clear causal "Therefore" between every major plot point?** The anomaly leads to investigation leads to resistance leads to discovery — each step causally connected.
- [x] **Do the forces of antagonism escalate throughout?** From minor data oddity to institutional pressure to personal threat.
- [ ] **Does the protagonist move from a passive to an active state?** This should happen at the Act I Climax. Before that, she's reactive (investigating because something is wrong). After page 80, she must make a choice — that's when she becomes active. Verify this transition is sharp.
- [ ] **Is the ending an absolute and irreversible change of values?** Cannot assess yet (Act I only), but the Act I Climax should be irreversible — she cannot go back to trusting the institution.

## Summary

The structural problem is not that the discovery happens on page 80. Page 80 is a reasonable location for an Act I Climax. The problem is that pages 1-79 contain no value turns, no escalating antagonism, and no pressure on the protagonist's Sacred Flaw. The fix is to reframe the discovery as the culmination of Act I rather than its beginning, and to fill the preceding pages with causally linked events that each turn the Trust/Betrayal value and crack her belief that institutions protect truth.
