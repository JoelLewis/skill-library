# MealSync Ghost Kitchen Expansion: Stakeholder Review Protocol

**Iron Law**: `NO FINAL VERSION WITHOUT DOCUMENTED STAKEHOLDER FEEDBACK AND RESOLUTION`

**Feedback Type Sought**: Coaching on strategy direction + Evaluation against investment criteria
(Source: Stone & Heen, *Thanks for the Feedback*, Ch. 2)

---

## Pre-read Package

Distribute to all reviewers 48 hours before the review session. The pre-read distills findings from all seven prior chain steps into a narrative arc: problem, market, competition, customer, financials, and risks.

### Section 1: The Strategic Question (from Problem Framing, Step 1)

MealSync faces a resource allocation decision, not a yes/no question. The framing is: *Given MealSync's $2M ARR base in restaurant inventory SaaS, what is the right level of investment in the ghost kitchen vertical, and under what conditions should we scale, hold, or exit?*

Key context for reviewers:
- Internal views are split. CEO sees a natural market extension; VP Sales sees a distraction from core restaurant growth.
- This is not a "bet the company" move at the proposed $800K investment level, but it is material relative to current ARR.

### Section 2: Stakeholder Map and Review Roles (from Stakeholder Discovery, Step 2)

| Stakeholder | Disposition | Role in Review | Feedback Type Expected |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CEO** | Champion (high enthusiasm for expansion) | Decision owner — final go/no-go authority | Evaluation: does the plan meet their growth ambitions? |
| **VP Sales** | Skeptic (concerned about core distraction) | Critical voice — must surface objections explicitly | Coaching: what conditions would make them support this? |
| **CTO** | Neutral, cost-concerned | Technical feasibility validator | Coaching: are development estimates realistic? |
| **Board** | Growth-focused | Investment committee — fiduciary review | Evaluation: does ROI meet the portfolio hurdle rate? |

**Purpose alignment script** (to open the session): "We are seeking two things today. First, coaching on the strategy — what would you change, add, or remove? Second, evaluation against our investment criteria — does this clear the bar? We are not seeking approval of a final plan; this is a draft designed to be improved by your input."
(Source: Stone & Heen, *Thanks for the Feedback*, Ch. 3)

### Section 3: Market Opportunity Summary (from Market Context, Step 3)

- Ghost kitchen market projected at $50B by 2028, but growth is volatile with consolidation pressure.
- The market is in a shakeout phase — early hype has given way to operator rationalization and unit economics scrutiny.
- **Timing signal for reviewers**: The window for software-layer entry is open precisely because real-estate-focused players are consolidating. Late entry into a consolidating market carries both opportunity (distressed customers looking for efficiency tools) and risk (fewer total operators).

### Section 4: Competitive Positioning (from Competitive Analysis, Step 4)

- CloudKitchens and Kitchen United are focused on real estate and kitchen-as-a-service models, not software.
- Inventory management is a gap in their offering — neither has built a dedicated inventory platform.
- **The strategic argument**: MealSync enters as a software complement, not a direct competitor to real estate players. This is a "picks and shovels" positioning during a real estate gold rush.
- **The counterargument VP Sales should stress-test**: If CloudKitchens or Kitchen United decide to build or acquire inventory capabilities, MealSync's niche collapses. What evidence exists that they will not do this?

### Section 5: Target Customer (from Buyer Persona, Step 5)

- Primary persona: multi-unit ghost kitchen operators running 3-10 locations.
- Job-to-be-done: centralized inventory visibility and waste reduction across distributed kitchens with no on-site management.
- **Struggling moment**: Operators currently cobble together spreadsheets and restaurant POS systems not designed for delivery-only models.
- **Implication for the business case**: Willingness to pay is anchored to waste reduction ROI, not feature richness. The sales motion is ROI-based, not feature-comparison.

### Section 6: Financial Summary (from Business Case, Step 6)

|---|---|

### Section 7: Risk Assessment (from Assumption Audit, Step 7)

The assumption audit flagged four items that require explicit stakeholder attention:

| Assumption | Confidence | Review Action Required |
|---|---|---|
| TAM estimate ($50B ghost kitchen market) | **Medium** | Board should assess whether this aligns with their own market intelligence |

## Review Session Protocol

### Logistics

- **Duration**: 90 minutes (15 min silent read if anyone has not read the pre-read, 60 min structured discussion, 15 min decision capture)
- **Scribe**: Designated note-taker records every decision, open question, and action item in real time
- **Format**: In-person or video with shared screen on the pre-read document

### Discussion Structure

**Block 1 (20 min) — Advice Round**
Each stakeholder answers: "What advice do you have for improving this plan?"
(Source: First Round Review, "The Best Leaders are Feedback Magnets" — "advice" framing lowers social friction and produces more actionable input)

Order: VP Sales first (the skeptic speaks before the champion to prevent anchoring), then CTO, then Board, then CEO last.

**Block 2 (20 min) — Named Conflicts**
Surface the specific tensions identified in prior steps:

1. **CEO vs. VP Sales on distraction risk**: The CEO sees ghost kitchens as a growth vector; VP Sales sees it pulling focus from the core $2M ARR base. The pre-read does not resolve this tension — the review must. Ask directly: "VP Sales, what conditions would change your assessment from 'distraction' to 'worthwhile bet'? CEO, what evidence would cause you to pull back?"

2. **Conversion rate gap**: The business case rests on a conversion rate assumption with low confidence. VP Sales owns pipeline data and must either validate or dispute this number during the session. If VP Sales cannot provide a defensible number, the plan must be revised to include a conversion rate discovery phase before full investment.

3. **Development cost variance**: CTO must confirm whether the $800K estimate includes ghost-kitchen-specific integrations (multi-location inventory sync, delivery platform APIs, virtual brand management). If not, the estimate is understated.

**Block 3 (20 min) — Decision Gate**
Three possible outcomes:
- **Proceed**: All four stakeholders agree the plan clears the bar (with documented modifications).
- **Revise and re-review**: Material changes are needed. Cycle back to Step 1 of the state machine (Prepare Pre-read) with specific revision instructions.
- **Kill**: The risk profile or strategic fit is insufficient. Document the reasoning for future reference.

### Safety Protocol

If the session moves toward silence (VP Sales disengages, stops pushing back) or violence (CEO dismisses objections, Board pressures for speed):

1. **Go Meta**: "It feels like we've stopped debating the real issue. Can we name what's making this hard?"
(Source: Johnson, *Scaling People*)

2. **Restore Mutual Purpose**: "We all want MealSync to make the highest-ROI use of the next $800K. That's the shared goal here — not winning the argument."
(Source: Grenny et al., *Crucial Conversations*, Ch. 5)

3. **Separate person from position**: If VP Sales's skepticism is being read as disloyalty, name it: "Disagreement on strategy is not disagreement on commitment. We need the skeptic's voice to make this decision well."

## Conflict Resolution Framework

### The Core Conflict: Growth Ambition vs. Focus Discipline

This is not resolvable by data alone. It is a strategic values question: does MealSync prioritize expanding TAM or deepening penetration in its current market? The review must produce a documented answer, not a compromise that avoids the question.

### Resolution Approach

**If the group agrees to proceed**, document:
- The specific conversion rate threshold that triggers a hold/exit decision
- The CTO-validated development budget (not the business case estimate — the reviewed estimate)
- The quarterly review cadence for ghost kitchen metrics separate from core business
- VP Sales's conditions for active support (not grudging compliance)

**If the group decides to revise**, document:
- What specific information is missing (most likely: conversion rate evidence)
- Who owns producing that information and by when
- The date for the re-review session

**If the group decides to kill**, document:
- The primary reason (market timing, resource constraint, strategic misfit)
- Under what future conditions MealSync would revisit the ghost kitchen vertical
- How to communicate the decision to the team without killing future strategic ambition

## Close-the-Loop Commitment

Within 48 hours of the review session, distribute:

1. **Decision summary**: The outcome (proceed / revise / kill) with the reasoning in 3-5 sentences
2. **Feedback log**: Every piece of stakeholder advice received, categorized as accepted, modified, or declined-with-reasoning
3. **Action items**: Owner, deliverable, and deadline for every open item
4. **Next milestone**: Either the launch timeline (if proceed), the re-review date (if revise), or the archival location for the analysis (if kill)

(Source: First Round Review — closing the loop gets stakeholders invested in the outcome)
